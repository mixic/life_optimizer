## 1a. "Muslim" as a census nationality — the documented administrative record

DOCUMENTED SEQUENCE (all internal Yugoslav acts; no external sponsor appears anywhere in the record):
- 1943, November: ZAVNOBiH (State Anti-Fascist Council for the National Liberation of Bosnia and
  Herzegovina), first session: Muslims treated as equal to Serbs and Croats. Days later the Second
  Session of AVNOJ classified Muslims only as a "distinct ethnic group" in Bosnia and Herzegovina;
  Sandžak and its Muslims were not mentioned. [SCHOLARLY, peer-reviewed]
  Azem Kožar, "HOD PO TRNJU: MUSLIMANI/BOŠNJACI OD ETNIČKE SKUPINE DO NACIJE" (A Walk Through
  Thorns: Muslims/Bosniaks from an Ethnic Group to a Nation), *Sandžački zbornik* No. 2 (2026),
  pp. 51–78, Bošnjačko nacionalno vijeće u Republici Srbiji.
  Abstract (read in full): https://www.ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1444222
  Kožar's summary: after 1943 the Muslims "were treated in the new constitutions as a national
  minority, with tendencies toward their assimilation into either Serbian or Croatian national
  identity. After two decades of struggle within socialist Yugoslavia, Muslims attained national
  status."
- 1960s: Bosnian Muslim communist intellectuals argued Muslims were a distinct native Slavic people
  deserving nation status. [SCHOLARLY, via tertiary]
- 1964: Fourth Congress of the Bosnian branch of the League of Communists of Yugoslavia.
- 1968: recognition of Bosnian Muslims as a distinct nation at a meeting of the Bosnian Central
  Committee. The Constitution was then amended to list "Muslims" in a national sense — a constitutive
  nation, but NOT under the Bosniak name (a compromise; the Bosnian Muslim communist leadership had
  preferred "Bosniak"). [Source for the 1964/1968 detail: tertiary, citing Ivo Banac, *The National
  Question in Yugoslavia: Origins, History, Politics* (Cornell UP, 1988), pp. 287–288.
  https://web.archive.org/web/20190405071248/https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims_by_nationality
  → THE 1964/1968 DATES ARE NOT YET VERIFIED AGAINST BANAC DIRECTLY. Flag.]
- 1971 census: "the status of 'nation' was conferred upon Yugoslav Muslims." [SCHOLARLY, read in full]
  Beverly Crawford, "Explaining Cultural Conflict in Ex-Yugoslavia," in Crawford & Lipschutz (eds),
  *The Myth of "Ethnic Conflict"*, UC Berkeley IAS Research Series No. 98, 1998.
  https://ciaotest.cc.columbia.edu/book/crawford/crawford06.html
  Crawford's explanation: the Chamber of Nationalities (upgraded 1963, again 1967) allocated
  representation by nation, so "Muslims could therefore be represented in the Chamber of Nationalities."
- 1971 figure: 1,729,932 declared as Muslims in the sense of nationality. [Tertiary/Greek doctoral
  thesis; NOT verified against the census volume] http://ikee.lib.auth.gr/record/134360/files/GRI-2014-12385.pdf
  Primary census methodology (Slovenian statistical office, 1971) — PDF not renderable this session:
  https://www.stat.si/publikacije/popisi/1971/1971_T_MET.pdf
- 1974 constitution: Bosnia's constitution treated Muslims, Serbs and Croats as the republic's
  constituent peoples. [SCHOLARLY, needs primary confirmation]
- Relevant scholarly monographs located but not read: Francine Friedman, *The Bosnian Muslims: Denial
  of a Nation* https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9780429496783/bosnian-muslims-francine-friedman
  ; Brenna Miller, "Between Faith and Nation: Defining Bosnian Muslims in Tito's Yugoslavia,
  1945–1980" (Ohio State diss.) https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/etd/r/1501/10?clear=10&p10_accession_num=osu1532003889562038
  ; Mitja Velikonja, *Religious Separation and Political Intolerance in Bosnia-Herzegovina* — search
  snippet: "The recognition of Muslim national identity is also linked to the dismissal of Aleksander
  Ranković and the politics he promoted" (Ranković removed 1966 — i.e. placed in the context of the
  fall of the Serbian hard-line faction, an internal party dynamic).

THE ONE GENUINELY EXTERNAL ACT IN THIS HISTORY (and it points the other way):
- Austro-Hungarian administration of Bosnia (1878–1918) officially endorsed *Bošnjaštvo*
  ("Bosniakhood") as the basis of a multi-confessional Bosnian nation — a policy that "aspired to
  isolate Bosnia and Herzegovina from its irredentist neighbors (Orthodox Serbia, Catholic Croatia,
  and the Muslims of the Ottoman Empire) and to negate the concept of Croatian and Serbian
  nationhood." [SCHOLARLY, via tertiary citing Donia & Fine 1994 and Velikonja 2003]
  https://web.archive.org/web/20190405071248/https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims_by_nationality
  CRITICAL POINT: this external imperial intervention sought to create a CROSS-CONFESSIONAL Bosnian
  identity, the opposite of creating a separate "Muslim" nationality. It is therefore NOT support for
  the thesis that an external power created the Muslim category to divide and rule.

VERDICT FOR FIELD 3/7: the creation of "Muslim" as a census nationality is a documented
administrative and constitutional act of the Yugoslav communist state, adopted after an internal
two-decade political and intellectual campaign, and is attributed by every source located to internal
Yugoslav actors. No source located attributes it to an external third party. The claim that an
outside power created the category to divide and conquer is, on the sources found, an interpretation
without documentary support.

## 1c. Historiography — the three camps and the debate

[SCHOLARLY, read in full] Beverly Crawford 1998 (URL above) identifies THREE rival camps:
1. Primordialist/essentialist ("ancient hatreds"). Crawford: essentialist arguments "are difficult to
   discredit because they are nonfalsifiable." Her counter-evidence: inter-ethnic marriage was rising
   in the decade before the war; the share identifying as "Yugoslav" rather than ethnically grew after
   Tito's death in 1980; as late as 1989 a majority of Serbs favoured a liberal federal Yugoslavia;
   Ante Marković, a Croat liberal, was the most popular politician in all six republics; Serbs in
   Serbia showed decreasing ethnic solidarity with Serbs in Krajina and Bosnia during the war.
2. International-level. Crawford reports the German-recognition accounts — including the explicit
   "divide-and-conquer" version: "The more sinister version of this claim was that given its new
   international power position, Germany was attempting to recreate its World War II alliance with an
   independent Croatia and impose a divide-and-conquer strategy in the Balkans to protect its
   interests and enhance its relative power in the region." She calls these accounts "both easily
   discredited," conceding recognition "clearly hastened the dissolution of Yugoslavia" but noting
   "the war had begun six months earlier."
   The most sophisticated international-level account: Susan Woodward, *Balkan Tragedy: Chaos and
   Dissolution after the Cold War* (Brookings, 1995) — IMF/conditionality and global integration
   eroding the federal institutions that had provided stability. Crawford's critique: Woodward's own
   evidence shows IMF policy was designed to STRENGTHEN federal institutions and was rejected by
   domestic forces; and the comparative cases (Bulgaria, Brazil) had debt and austerity without
   cultural violence.
3. Instrumentalist/elite-manipulation. Named by Crawford: Milošević, Tudjman and local politicians and
   intellectuals who "exploited ethnic differences and whipped up ethnic hatred in their effort to
   expand their own power base." She cites Laura Silber & Allan Little, *The Death of Yugoslavia*.
   Canonical monograph: V.P. Gagnon, *The Myth of Ethnic War: Serbia and Croatia in the 1990s*
   (Cornell UP, 2004). https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.7591/9780801468889/html
   Dejan Jović, *Yugoslavia: A State That Withered Away* (Purdue UP, 2009).
   https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/slavic-review/article/abs/yugoslavia-a-state-that-withered-away-by-dejan-jovic-central-european-studies-west-lafayette-purdue-university-press-2009-xii-419-pp-notes-bibliography-index-4995-paper/C7704D77C364FAB1F0FD920450128035
   Robert Kaplan, *Balkan Ghosts* (1993) is the standard reference for the "ancient hatreds" position.
   The e-IR article "Were 'Ancient Hatreds' the Primary Cause of the Yugoslavian Civil War" is at
   https://www.e-ir.info/pdf/78802 but that URL now 404s/redirects — MUST BE RE-LOCATED.
   Hoover Institution, "Beyond 'Ancient Hatreds'": https://www.hoover.org/research/beyond-ancient-hatreds
   (fetched; content was site navigation only — NOT usable.)

Crawford's institutional record (the documented internal drivers) [SCHOLARLY synthesis]:
- 1946 constitution: non-centralised federalism; centre could decide only in narrow areas without
  constituent-unit approval.
- 1963 constitution: further decentralisation to communes; Chamber of Nationalities given legislative
  power, upgraded again 1967 to senior chamber. Consequence: federal deputies became accountable to
  republican assemblies, shifting loyalty from centre to republic.
- Ethnic balancing: 20 delegates per republic regardless of population. Serbs ~40% of population but
  ~14% of Chamber votes; Slovenes 8.5% of population, 8.5% of votes. Crawford: this "channelled
  resentments … toward specific ethnic groups."
- 1965 economic reform: investment funds to republican banks; tax-raising authority to republics.
- 1974 constitution: "a watershed that turned Yugoslavia into a de facto confederation"; federal
  powers reduced to foreign policy, defence, national rights and minimum economic instruments, all by
  consensus; equal numbers per republic in federal posts; Kosovo and Vojvodina raised to parity;
  eight-member collective presidency.
- Economic record: inter-republican trade fell from 27.7% to 23.1% of social product 1970–76; by 1981,
  66% of trade intraregional, 22% interregional, 4% of investment crossing republican borders; 1982
  real gross fixed investment fell 37%; average annual growth fell to 0.9% from 6.3%; inflation
  reached 100% annually by mid-decade; unemployment rose from 600,000 (1982) to 912,000 (1983).
- JNA: officer corps disproportionately Serb — 40% of population, 65% of officers by the late 1980s;
  by 1990 the rank and file "rapidly turned into a virtually all-Serb force."

[JUDICIAL] ICJ, *Bosnia and Herzegovina v. Serbia and Montenegro*, Judgment 26 February 2007.
Summary (read in full): https://www.icj-cij.org/node/103898 | Case: https://www.icj-cij.org/case/91
Holdings:
- Srebrenica: acts of genocide, committed by members of the VRS from about 13 July 1995.
- Jurisdiction: the Court "has no power to rule on alleged breaches of other obligations under
  international law, not amounting to genocide… even if the alleged breaches are of obligations under
  peremptory norms." => The Court COULD NOT and DID NOT rule on aggression or on the triggering of
  the war.
- Attribution FAILED: not attributable via organs (RS/VRS were not de jure FRY organs); not "complete
  dependence"; no "effective control" over the specific operations; no instructions from Belgrade
  proved; complicity not established because it was not conclusively shown the decision to kill was
  brought to Belgrade's attention.
- BUT Serbia WAS found in breach of the obligation to PREVENT genocide: the FRY was in "a position of
  influence … unlike that of any of the other States parties"; it "could hardly have been unaware of
  the serious risk" once the VRS decided to occupy the enclave; bound by the Court's 1993 provisional
  measures Orders (esp. 8 April 1993).
- Also found responsible for failing to punish (transfer Mladić) — confirm exact dispositif wording
  against the full judgment before quoting.
=> For Field 4: a foreign state WAS held to account before the ICJ for the Bosnian war — but only
under the Genocide Convention, only for prevention/punishment, not for triggering the war, and not
for the genocide itself.

RWANDA — IMPORTANT CORRECTION received from the research subagent, to be reflected in drafting:
The common claim that "Belgium introduced ethnic identity CARDS in Rwanda in 1933" is IMPRECISE.
Léon Saur, "Rwanda: Identity Papers under Belgian Colonial Occupation," in Awenengo Dalberto & Banégas
(eds), *Identification and Citizenship in Africa* (Routledge, 2021), pp. 160–165.
https://shs.hal.science/halshs-03945201v1
Saur: "In Belgian-occupied Rwanda, there were identity booklets, never identity cards. These only
appeared after independence. Massively distributed from the second half of the 1930 onwards as part
of the first general census of Hommes adultes valides (HAV) carried out in Ruanda-Urundi, these
booklets of 16 to 24 pages … The 'racial-ethnic' affiliation of the holder is therefore one piece of
information among many others. It appears on the fourth page. Moreover, Rwandans were able to develop
avoidance and circumvention strategies, so that changes in 'racial-ethnic' affiliation were possible
until independence, and even long afterwards."
=> booklets not cards; mass distribution tied to the HAV census; ethnicity one datum among many;
category porous and changeable. The porosity strengthens the "fluidity" side of the debate.
