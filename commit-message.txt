Widen the deduction engine to property and pensioners, and fix two more collisions

Closes the two largest gaps the engine reported, and the diagnostic used to check
the new code found two further bugs. Test count rises from 186 to 194.

Property: the base matters more than the rate

Every property rule in the ESTV export is a percentage of the IMPUTED RENTAL VALUE
(`Eigenmietwert`), not of income: `Abzug vom Eigenmietwert` is 20-40% of it and
maintenance 10-20%. The engine applied them to gross income, which is a category
error rather than a rounding difference -- a CHF 100,000 earner with a CHF 20,000
rental value had maintenance calculated off their salary.

`Household::homeowner(value)` supplies the fact, and three things follow:

  - a tenant gets nothing, because every property rule is gated on the value
  - the deduction scales with the HOME, not the salary -- asserted directly by
    doubling each in turn and checking which one moves the figure
  - property deductions are capped at the rental value: you cannot deduct more
    maintenance against a home than the home is deemed to earn

The Eigenmietwert is also an INCOME ADDITION, not only a deduction base

The module doc flagged this as a gap and it was a real one: the rental value the
owner would otherwise have paid themselves is added to taxable income first, and
only then is maintenance deducted. Modelling the deduction alone understates a
homeowner's taxable income.

`DeductionAssessment::income_addition` now carries it and
`taxable_income_with_addition()` applies both sides. At CHF 100,000 with a
CHF 20,000 rental value, taxable income lands ABOVE the salary despite the
deductions -- the correct direction, and the one a one-sided model gets wrong. The
addition is kept as its own field rather than netted into `total()` because the
two are different lines on an assessment, and a caller that ignored the addition
would silently understate the tax.

Pensioner deductions, which were classified as the wrong thing

`Abzug für AHV/IV-Rentner` is 10 flat rules plus 15 phase-out scales across eight
cantons. They were classified as MEANS-TESTED, which excluded them from every
household and would have applied the wrong mechanism to a pensioner one.

They are now their own category behind `Household::pensioner()`. The default is
`false` rather than unknown, deliberately: granting these by omission would be a
large silent error for every working household, and 25 rules is far too much to
concede. The flat-amount form is applied (SZ 4,000, GL 2,100, SO 5,000); the
PERCENTAGE form is not, because Basel-Landschaft's is 40-60% of the PENSION and
this model carries no pension income -- deducting a share of a salary would be a
share of the wrong base.

Two more bugs the diagnostic found

  Both property-maintenance age bands summed
    "mit Alter bis 10 Jahren" and "über 10 Jahren" are bands of ONE deduction
    keyed on the building's age, and a building has one age. Summing them
    deducted CHF 6,000 against a CHF 20,000 rental value.

  Abzug Vermögensverwaltungskosten applied to income
    A WEALTH deduction sitting under `Steuerart = Einkommen` in the export, which
    is how it came to deduct 0.2-0.3% of a SALARY as a wealth-management cost in
    ZH, SZ, OW, NW and GL. This model has no wealth, so the rule is reported as
    not applicable instead of applied to the wrong base.

Both are regression tests now. The pattern is familiar enough to name: the export
lists ALTERNATIVES AS SEPARATE ROWS, and a model that sums rows instead of
grouping variants overstates every family that has them. It has now bitten four
times -- child age brackets, pillar 3a solution type, property age bands, and
pensioner groups.
