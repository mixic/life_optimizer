Add a sourced deduction engine, and fix five selection bugs it exposed

`src/deductions.rs` assesses a household against the imported ESTV rules, so the
invented ~35%-cap estimate in `tax.rs` now has a sourced counterpart to be
compared against. Test count rises from 175 to 186.

The arithmetic is trivial; the work is selection

The data holds 574 deduction rules per jurisdiction -- every deduction a canton
offers. A taxpayer takes a SUBSET, so summing them would understate taxable income
far more badly than the 35% cap overstates it. Selection is therefore explicit:
each rule gets a `RuleCategory` from a closed list, a `Household` states the facts
a category depends on, an unknown fact skips the category rather than assuming it,
mutually exclusive variants are grouped so at most one applies, and anything
unclassified is REPORTED rather than applied.

Five bugs, all found by comparing output rather than by reasoning

Every one produced a plausible number rather than an error, which is why the
module was built with a diagnostic beside the existing estimate from the start.
Each is now a regression test:

  Verheiratetenabzug on a single taxpayer            CHF 2,800 too much
  all three child age brackets summed                37.4% of income deducted
  secondary-employment expenses with no such income  CHF 2,400 too much
  pillar 3a tied AND untied variants summed          CHF 7,056 deducted twice
  contains("Kind") matched "ohne Kind"               disabled FR's and VS's
                                                     single-person deductions

The last is the same class of mistake as reading `Kantons-Id` as `Kanton`:
`"ohne Kind"` contains `"Kind"`, so a keyword test demanded children for a scale
that explicitly excludes them. Child conditions had to be parsed as conditions
rather than searched for as keywords. The `Alleinerziehende oder Verheiratete`
scale had a parallel flaw: "Alleinerziehende" is a single parent, not a married
household, so gating it on `married` was wrong in the other direction.

What the two models produce

At CHF 100,000 gross for a household declaring nothing optional:

  Bund  single              sourced  3,000 ( 3.0%)  estimate 15,700 (15.7%)
  Bund  married, 2 children sourced 12,600 (12.6%)  estimate 15,700 (15.7%)
  ZH    single              sourced  3,300 ( 3.3%)  estimate 15,700 (15.7%)
  ZH    married, 2 children sourced 12,600 (12.6%)  estimate 28,300 (28.3%)
  AG    single              sourced  3,000 ( 3.0%)  estimate 15,700 (15.7%)
  AG    married, 2 children sourced 15,400 (15.4%)  estimate 28,300 (28.3%)

The estimate is roughly FIVE TIMES the sourced figure for a single earner. It
overstates deductions and therefore understates tax -- an error in the taxpayer's
favour, which is part of why it survived this long without anyone questioning it.

The sourced figure is lower for a real reason, not an omission: this household
declares no insurance premiums, no pillar 3a, no childcare and no commuting costs,
and the engine refuses to invent them. With all four declared plus a second income
the federal total at CHF 100,000 becomes 42,056 (42.1%), with pillar 3a,
childcare, commuting and the second-earner deduction each itemised -- so the model
does reach high totals when a household actually has the facts to support them.

The tax base is deliberately NOT switched

`tax.rs` still uses the estimate. Changing it would move every number the tool
produces, and that should be a visible decision made against a stated comparison
rather than smuggled into the same commit as the engine. The module doc comment
says so, and SWISS_TAX_DATA.md section 4a records the table above.

Also fixed

  - Means-tested scales now match for single low earners in FR and VS, which the
    `"ohne Kind"` bug had disabled entirely.
  - Totals are capped at gross income, with `uncapped_total()` exposing the
    difference: Valais's phase-out table legitimately allows CHF 21,250, which
    exceeds a CHF 15,000 income.
  - `Schwellwert` rows classify into the means-tested family rather than counting
    as unclassified, so the `unclassified()` list surfaces rows that genuinely
    need attention instead of being filled with known parameters.

Limitations, stated rather than hidden

  - Property, Eigenmietwert and pensioner deductions are not applied and are
    reported in `skipped()`; Eigenmietwert also has a matching income ADDITION
    that is not modelled, so a homeowner's position is incomplete both ways.
  - Within a mutually exclusive family the larger amount is taken when the
    distinguishing fact is not held (a child's age, a tied pension solution), and
    the alternative is reported as skipped so the uncertainty is visible.
  - Means-tested scales are applied to income as given, not income net of the
    other deductions; that circularity is unresolved.
