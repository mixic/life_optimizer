Document that the estimate's rent component is unsourced and dominates it

No behaviour change. Records a finding that decides what the tax-base comparison
actually means, and pins it with a test so any future change is a decision rather
than an accident. Test count rises from 206 to 207.

The finding

`TaxDeductionBreakdown::rent` is 12% of gross (11% married), capped at 20,000. Rent
is **not deductible for a Swiss employee** -- there is no such deduction in cantonal
or federal law, and this component has no source.

It is not merely misnamed. The only space costs the ESTV export publishes are Vaud's
and Zug's `Maximalabzug Miete`, and both are FLAT CEILINGS that do not vary with
income:

    VD  6,800 / 11,000 single / 13,500 married
    ZG  10,800

This component instead scales at 12% of gross, passing every real ceiling by
CHF 100,000 and rising without limit -- reaching CHF 20,000 at CHF 200,000, above
every published cap. No sourced rule behaves that way.

It also dominates the estimate: 71-76% of the whole total for a household with no
children (CHF 4,800 of CHF 6,280 at 40k; CHF 12,000 of CHF 15,700 at 100k), because
every other component caps at a comparatively low figure. So it is the single
largest reason the estimate and `src/deductions.rs` disagree.

A correction to my own first draft

I initially wrote that removing it would cut the estimate by "31-72%". Measuring
instead of estimating gave 71-76% for a childless household and revealed that the
earlier figure conflated `rent` with `family_specific`. The documented numbers are
the measured ones.

Why this is documented rather than changed

Removing it would raise every affected household's tax and move recommendations --
the same class of change as the tax-base fix, but without that fix's justification.
The tax-base defect was arithmetic contradicting the published table; this is a
modelling decision about whether to keep an invented component, and the reported
figures are what the tool's output has always meant. It is the user's call, now with
the size of the decision stated.

Documentation

SWISS_TAX_DATA.md records it beside the withdrawn `non_deductible_total`, and
`rent_component_is_unsourced_and_scales_with_income` pins the contrast with the
sourced ceilings -- if it ever stops scaling with income, or stops dominating, the
documented magnitude is stale and the test says so.

Verified: 207 tests pass, clippy unchanged at 9 pre-existing warnings.
