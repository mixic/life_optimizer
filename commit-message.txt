Show the sourced deduction model beside the estimate it will replace

The two deduction models disagree by roughly a factor of five for a single earner,
and they disagree in a direction that matters: the hand-entered ~35%-cap estimate
overstates deductions, so it UNDERSTATES tax. That is worth seeing on a real
scenario rather than only in a test fixture, so `optimize` now prints both.

The sourced model is still NOT used for the reported figures -- the comparison
line says so explicitly. Switching the tax base moves every number the tool
produces, and that should be a deliberate change made after looking at this
output, not a side effect of adding the engine.

Every figure is itemised and tagged with the jurisdiction it came from, because a
total with no breakdown cannot be checked against the rules it claims to use. The
count of rules considered and not applied is printed for the same reason: a small
total should read as "these facts were not supplied", not as "there is nothing to
deduct". That distinction is the whole design of the engine.

`canton_code_from_basis` maps the display label back to a jurisdiction code. An
unrecognised label yields no cantonal rows rather than a guessed canton, and the
Bern default is recognised from its `official Bern table` label.
